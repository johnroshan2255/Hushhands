# The Thread-Bearers: the story

A wordless horror tale in black silhouette, told through what you see, short story cards between
chapters, and the things you find. About two hours for someone who knows every answer; days for
someone who does not, because the Loom kills you while it teaches you.

How it is to be built, chapter by chapter, is in `DESIGN.md`.

---

## The world: the Loom

Under the ground there is a weaving-house so large it has weather. Its mills, vats, granaries and
towers were built by the **Weaver**, an old, long-fingered thing that made all the cloth the world
above ever wore. The Loom hangs from the underside of the world like a spider's egg sac: its lower
halls stand on the ground, but its upper halls **hang upside down** from the roof of the earth, and
the great drum at its middle still turns them now and then, so that up becomes down.

At the very top (or the very bottom; in the Loom you can't always tell) stands the **Great
Spindle**. While it turns, a pale light falls through the Loom's cracks, and things made of thread
hold together.

The Spindle has stopped. The light is thinning. Everything that eats cloth is waking up: moths,
rats, rot, and worse things that were only ever kept down by the light.

## Who you are

Small sackcloth dolls, the last ones the Weaver stitched. Each of you has a single **amber thread**
sewn into your waist, the last of the Weaver's own light, and it ties you to each other. Where it
runs, it glows. Tied, you can hold each other over drops, swing, climb each other and trip giants.
Tied, you also share one life: if one of you dies, the thread goes cold for all.

Alone, you wake tied to a **Spool-Lantern**, a wooden spool with a candle in it. It cannot walk,
but it can be set down, hung up and called back. It is the second body you do not have.

**The dolls bleed.** The Weaver filled them with stuffing soaked in **red dye**, so that they
would look alive. When a doll is torn, it bleeds red and spills its stuffing like a body spills its
insides. The creatures of the Loom bleed **black ink**. That is the gore of this game: everything
is cloth, dye and ink, and it is shown without holding back (see "How death looks").

## What you want

To carry the thread up through the whole Loom to the Great Spindle and wind it, so that the light
comes back and the things in the dark go back to sleep.

That is what the opening tells you. It is not the whole truth.

## The mysteries

The game never explains itself. These are the questions it plants, and where the answers are hidden.

1. **The Other Doll.** From the first chapter you keep seeing a doll *ahead* of you: far off in
   the fog, crossing a light shaft, going through a door just before it shuts. It is never caught.
   It leaves scratched drawings on the walls. With friends, there is one Other Doll for each of
   you, and each wears a patch the same colour as one of yours.
   *Answer (Ch. 14, the cages):* the Other Dolls are you, from the last time. Their threads are
   cut. They were winding the Spindle when the light last failed.
2. **The tally marks.** Scratched into the walls of every lantern room, groups of five. The
   further up you go, the more there are. *Answer:* each one is a doll that reached that lantern.
   The count at the Spindle is not a small number.
3. **Whose light falls through the cracks?** Pale shafts fall onto the ground all through the Loom.
   *Answer (Ch. 15):* daylight. The outside. No doll has ever reached it.
4. **What the Spindle wants.** *Answer:* not your thread. You. Dolls are its wicks. The Weaver
   made you to be burned, so that the Loom would go on.
5. **Where the Weaver is.** Its hand passes behind the fog in Acts I and II, closer each time.
   *Answer:* in the tower, being eaten alive by the Moth Mother, and still stitching dolls with what
   fingers it has left.
6. **The buttons.** Twelve lost buttons, the eyes of earlier dolls, hidden off the main path. Each
   comes with a card telling how its doll died. Together, the cards tell what the Weaver did, and
   why, and they open the third ending.
7. **After the end.** Finish once, and the Loom starts again (*Second Thread*): the cages hold
   dolls in your colours, the Other Dolls are gone because you are them now, and some of the
   drawings on the walls are new. They are the ones *you* would have scratched.

## The ending: a choice

At the top, with the Spindle in front of you and the Moth Mother behind you, you choose:

- **Wind.** You give yourselves to the Spindle. You burn. The light returns; the Loom lives;
  somewhere at the bottom, a new doll wakes in the scrap pit, tied to a lantern. *(THE TURNING)*
- **Cut.** You cut the thread at the top and climb out through the crack into the daylight. Behind
  you the Loom goes dark and unravels: the monsters, the caged dolls, the Weaver, all of it.
  *(OUTSIDE)*
- **Unpick** (secret). With all twelve buttons, the Weaver can see again. It unpicks its own
  stitches instead of yours: the cages open, every doll walks out into the light with you, and
  the Loom winds down gently. *(THE WHOLE CLOTH)*

With several players the choice is made together: everyone pulls toward one side. If the group
splits, the thread snaps, and the ending goes to whoever was pulling hardest. Those on the losing
side are pulled with them.

The run titles stay as how you got there: **The Whole Doll / Unbroken** (no falls),
**Mended** (falls, never cut), **The Severed Cord** (the thread was cut on the way).

## How death looks

Every death is quick, clear and specific to what killed you, shown in silhouette with the red
dye as the only colour besides the thread. It must be terrible to watch and teach you in one look
what you did wrong. The thread goes cold, the screen holds on it for a beat, and you are back.

- **Jaw trap:** snaps shut at the waist; the doll folds; red sprays across the grass.
- **The shears:** cut a doll in two at the thread line; both halves fall; the thread still glows in
  one of them.
- **The Needle-Crawler:** the needle goes through and lifts the doll, kicking, into the dark.
- **Drowning:** the doll's stuffing soaks heavy; it sinks slowly, reaching up; bubbles, then not.
- **A Head-Worm takes you:** the doll stops fighting, stands up straight, and its head splits open.
- **Drinkers:** the abdomen fills red as the doll goes grey and flat as an empty sack.
- **Rats:** the swarm closes over; when it moves on, only the thread is left on the floor.
- **Rot:** the doll darkens, sags and comes apart at its seams, stuffing falling out like mould.
- **The Glutton:** the floor opens; one bite; stuffing rains back up through the boards.
- **Presses:** flat, with a burst of red.
- **Moths:** holes open in the doll until there is not enough left to stand.
- **A falling turn (the drum):** dropped the full height of a room that just turned over.

**What you leave behind.** Where you died, your body stays for the rest of the chapter: a torn doll
with a cold thread. Die often in one place and the floor there fills with your own bodies. With
friends, everyone's bodies stay. The group can see, on the next try, exactly where it went wrong.

**Gore setting** *(see DESIGN.md)*: **Full** (red dye, dismemberment) or **Stitched** (grey stuffing,
no red, deaths cut away sooner).

## The journey: 5 acts, 15 chapters

Each chapter ends at a lantern where the game saves. Lanterns inside a chapter are checkpoints.
Times are for someone who knows the answers; a first time is three to five times longer.

### ACT I: THE BOTTOM OF THE LOOM *(waking up; the thread is life)* ~25 min

**1. The Scrap Pit** *(built)*
**Cold open:** before you can move, another doll runs past you in the fog. Something long reaches
down and takes it, and red rains onto the scrap. Then you wake properly. The thread pulls tight for
the first time. Pressure plates, the first gate. The Other Doll, far ahead, goes through it first.
*Card: "The Spindle has stopped. Carry the thread to it."*

**2. The Meadow of Pins** *(built, to be extended)*
Grass full of jaw traps, some sunk out of sight. The first worm bed. The first death: yours. A
dead doll lies in a trap with its thread still faintly glowing. The first tally marks.

**3. The Chasm and the Burrowers** *(built, to be extended)*
Swinging on the thread over the drop. The worm tunnel: a Head-Worm gets into a doll and walks it
at its friends. You learn that the thread that saves you can also drag you down.
*Card: "Something was in your head. It wanted the others."*

### ACT II: THE MILL *(the working parts of the Loom, flooded and rotting)* ~30 min

**4. The Den of the Needle-Crawler** *(built)*
A blind thing on the ceiling with a needle for a mouth. Creep, keep still.

**5. The Drowned Mill** *(new)*
The mill has flooded and it is still filling. Water rises behind you, and you hold your breath
under it. **The Cistern:** a sealed room whose door locks behind you while water pours in; the way
out is a puzzle that must be solved before the water reaches the roof. **Eels** in the black water:
when one touches a wet thread, the shock runs along it to everyone tied. **Leeches** cling to
whoever lingers. A waterwheel turned by your weight.
*Find: the first button, in the mouth of a drowned doll.*

**6. The Gear Room** *(built)*
The Loom's clockwork, still turning. Riding cogs, timing jumps, and now a press that comes down on
the beat.

**7. The Fen of Drinkers** *(new)*
A marsh where the dye runoff collects. **Drinkers**, mosquitoes the size of dogs, hunt by warmth
and light, and the thread is both. Smother your glow in mud to hide; lure them into candle flames.
A Drinker that has fed is too heavy to fly high and can be ridden across the water, if you can
stand what it fed on.
*Card: "They are drawn to the thread. To what keeps you alive."*

### ACT III: THE HOUSE OF APPETITES *(the parts of the Loom where things eat)* ~30 min

**8. The Granary of Gnawers** *(new)*
Silos full of **rats**: a living flood that pours around anything dark. Light holds them back, and
the lantern's light is small. Rats chew thread: slack thread on the floor is eaten through. Keep
it taut, keep it lit, keep moving.

**9. The Dye-Works** *(new)*
Here the red dye was made: vats of it, and of poison. Fumes to hold your breath through.
**Rot-blooms** burst into spores; a doll that breathes them rots, and the rot creeps *along the
thread* to whoever is tied. Wash in clean water, or cut the rotting one loose. You find the vat
of red and understand what is inside you.
*Card: "The rot went down the thread. Into the others."*

**10. The Larder** *(new)*
A cold kitchen with something underneath it. **The Glutton** is a mouth in the floor that rises
toward anything that makes the boards shake. Hide in sacks, feed it what hangs from the hooks (the
hooks hold dolls), and pass while it chews. Bigger groups shake the boards more.

### ACT IV: THE UPTURNED LOOM *(where up and down stop meaning anything)* ~20 min

**11. The Marionette Theatre** *(new)*
An old puppet stage where the **Puppeteer** lives in the rafters and drops **grubs**: a grub on a
doll's head *swaps that doll's controls* (left is right, jump is creep), and the Puppeteer pulls
grubbed dolls on its own strings. Some doors only open for a puppet on the stage, so sometimes one
of you has to *let* it in. When you cut the Puppeteer down, its strings, which hold up more than
the stage, go slack, and the whole theatre swings over.
*Card: "It held the house up by its strings. It let go."*

**12. The Upturned Loom** *(new: gravity)*
The halls that hang from the roof of the earth, upside down. Everything works the other way:
- **You walk on the ceiling.** The floor is far above; falling means falling *up*.
- **Water lies on the roof** above you as a hanging lake, and when the drum turns, it falls.
- **The drum turns.** On a deep groan the whole hall rotates over. Everything loose falls to the
  new floor: crates, water, rats, bodies. Whoever is **holding fast** (SHIFT) stays where they
  are and lands on their feet; whoever is not falls the full height of the room. The thread
  becomes a rope between ceiling and floor.
- **Moth-dust.** Pale dust from the tower above makes a doll *weightless* for a few seconds; it
  floats up and away, held down only by the thread and whoever is holding it.
- **Creatures that live upside down**: a Needle-Crawler that hangs from the floor and stabs *up*;
  rats that pour down the walls when the room turns.
Puzzles use the turn: put the water where you need it before the turn, stand where the new floor
will be, ride falling crates, hold a weightless friend as a kite to reach a high door.

### ACT V: THE CLIMB *(up to the Spindle)* ~25 min

**13. The Glass Bridge and the Blind Cathedral** *(built)*
Thin glass that carries only so many. Bells that must not ring; the blind Choir sleeping under them.
The shears.

**14. The Collector's Yard** *(built)*
The giant that gathers dolls for the Spindle. You trip it with the thread. Its cages are full of
dolls, all of them tied, and among them, hanging by their cut threads, the Other Dolls. They wear
your colours.

**15. The Moth Tower and the Great Spindle** *(new)*
The last climb up the inside of the Spindle's tower, which turns as you climb (the drum again, now
a chase), with **moths** that eat cloth (and you are cloth) and the **Moth Mother** filling the
shaft below. At the top: the Weaver, half eaten, still stitching. The Spindle. The crack of
daylight. The choice.

## The lost buttons

Twelve buttons, one in each of twelve chapters (2 to 14, not 4), each off the main path and harder
than the way forward. Each card is one line about the doll that lost it ("It held the lever while
the others ran. They did not come back for it."). With friends, buttons belong to the group.

## How the story is told

- **No dialogue, no narrator in the world.** Silhouettes, light, sound.
- **A card between chapters:** one or two lines, white on black, like the opening.
- **Things you see:** dead dolls with their threads still glowing; the Other Doll ahead; tally
  marks; scratched drawings of the Spindle; the cages; the Weaver's hand in the fog.
- **The i panel** keeps the story so far: the cards seen, the buttons found, the drawings copied.
